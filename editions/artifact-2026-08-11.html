The One Thing

Sunday's edition ran under the headline "The market that stopped fearing the Fed." Forty-eight hours and one 5% oil spike later, that fear is back on the table — which tells you exactly how thin the relief was.

The consensus you carried into 2026 — soft landing, synchronised rate cuts, a docile Fed — is dead, and the tape has not fully told you yet. We now live in an oil-shock, inflation-resurgence regime: Brent is back at $88 on a fresh Iran/Russia supply scare, gold prints $4,340 and copper has broken $12,000 a tonne for the first time in history, and the three central banks that matter — a Warsh-led Fed, the ECB and the BoJ — are all in hawkish-hold-to-hiking mode. Markets are pricing a ~55% chance the Fed HIKES in September. And yet, in the same week, US July payrolls came in at minus 23,000 with 103k of downward revisions. That contradiction — a labour market visibly cracking while policymakers lean into an oil-driven price scare — is the single most important thing on your desk this morning. India sits downstream of all of it, and the swing variable, as ever, is the barrel.

For India specifically: the domestic engine is running hot — SBI and LIC just posted excellent quarters, July GST clocked ₹2.11 lakh crore (+15.4%), passenger-vehicle wholesales rose 33.6%, and the RBI upgraded FY27 GDP to 6.7%. But foreigners have pulled ~₹2.41 lakh crore out of Indian equities year-to-date — already more than all of 2025 — and Nifty is grinding sideways just below 24,600 rather than breaking down. That resilience under that much selling is the story most desks are underweighting. Below: what changed, where I think the crowd is wrong, and how the day sets up.

The set-up. Expect a weak-to-flat open. GIFT Nifty at ~24,616 is a shade below fair value, and the overnight story is oil: Brent pushed to $88 and WTI to $82.45 — the highest since 31 July — after Monday's ~5% rip, as hopes of a US–Iran de-escalation faded again. That is unambiguously the wrong wind for an oil-importing market. Wall Street closed at record highs (S&P 500 7,758, a fresh all-time high Friday), which usually flatters risk appetite here, but the transmission this morning is dominated by the barrel, not by beta.

Flows — read the tape correctly. On Monday (10 Aug) FPIs were net sellers of ₹1,290 crore in the cash market while DIIs absorbed it with ₹1,975 crore of net buying. That single day is the whole year in miniature: foreigners leaving, domestics catching. YTD FII outflows of ~₹2.41 lakh crore already exceed 2025's full-year ₹1.66 lakh crore, and still the index will not break. The SIP-fed domestic bid plus insurance and pension buying is now structurally deep enough to clear very large foreign selling without a price collapse. This is a genuine change in Indian market microstructure versus five years ago, and it is why "FIIs are dumping India" is a lazy read — the marginal price-setter has moved onshore.

Positioning. FIIs are carrying a large net-short index-futures book (~1.52 lakh contracts) — a hedged/cautious posture rather than an outright capitulation, which means a positive catalyst (a soft July CPI tomorrow, an oil reversal) could force a short-cover squeeze. Watch 24,500 as immediate support and 24,650–24,750 as the resistance shelf; a decisive close above 24,780 reopens the 25,000 conversation, while a break of 24,450 on an oil spike targets 24,250.

The event that matters this week is tomorrow. India's July CPI prints Wednesday 12 August, alongside US CPI the same day. June was 4.38%; the Street pencils in 4.4–4.5% for July, with food the upside risk and the late-July oil move a lurking threat to August's number. A print at or below 4.4% keeps the October RBI rate-cut option alive and would be a relief rally trigger; a 4.6%+ surprise, with Brent back at $88, effectively closes the easing door for the year. The RBI held at 5.25% (neutral) on 5 August and is currently draining, not adding, liquidity — running VRRR auctions to soak up a system surplus that topped ₹3 trillion. That is a quiet hawkish tell: Governor Malhotra wants "greater clarity on inflation" before he moves.

Where I break with the crowd

The bearish India-flows narrative is fighting the last war. The ₹2.41 lakh crore of FII outflow is being framed as a verdict on India — expensive, over-owned, earnings-light. Some of that is fair (India is not cheap at ~21x forward). But the larger driver is global and mechanical: a hawkish DM rate regime, a repatriation impulse when US cash yields 3.75% and the dollar is a coin-flip, and a rotation toward newly-cheap China and record-momentum US megacaps. Read that way, India's sideways grind under record selling is bullish information, not bearish — it tells you the domestic bid is winning. The pain trade from here is a rupee-stabilising, oil-cooling window that brings foreigners back into an index that never gave them a lower re-entry. I would not be positioned for an India breakdown; I would be positioned for a violent mean-reversion higher the moment oil rolls over.

02Sector Sweep

Banks & Financials — the quarter's clear winner

SBI (reported 7 Aug) is the read of the week: standalone PAT +10.2% to ₹21,121 cr, NII +14.9%, gross advances +18.6% to ₹50.5 lakh crore, and gross NPAs down to 1.47% from 1.83%. An 18%+ loan-growth print from the system's largest lender with improving asset quality is about as clean a mid-cycle credit signal as you get, and it de-risks the PSU-bank re-rating thesis. LIC (6 Aug) was arguably better on quality: PAT +22.8% to ₹13,492 cr but the story is VNB +61% and a VNB margin jumping 750bps to 22.9% on a decisive non-par mix shift — this is the operating leverage bulls have waited three years for. Flag: a widely-circulated PNB Q1 growth figure (+214%) looks inconsistent with its base and Bank of Baroda's reported ~72% YoY profit drop needs a one-off explanation — treat both as unverified until the filings are parsed.

Autos & EV — festive front-loading, Tata's EV inflection

July wholesales roared: industry PV dispatches +33.6% YoY to 4.63 lakh units, festive channel-stocking pulling volume forward. Maruti crossed 2 lakh domestic units in a single month for the first time ever (200,123). Tata Motors total +58.7% to 63,760, with a record 15,217 EV wholesales (+114%) — the first time it has cleared 15k EVs, a genuine inflection worth respecting given how bearish the Street has turned on the group. The risk, as always with a stocking-led month, is that sell-in is running ahead of sell-out; watch September retail (registrations) to confirm demand is real and not just inventory sitting in dealer yards.

IT Services — modest growth, big AI deal-books

The July prints (context, not this week) set a constructive tone: Infosys raised FY27 revenue guidance to 1.5–3.0% and flagged record large-deal TCV, with AI services now 8.2% of revenue; TCS pointed to a $9.5bn order book anchored by an AI-led SKF deal and a ~$2.6bn AI run-rate. The sector is not accelerating so much as proving it isn't being disintermediated by AI — the deal-books are the tell that clients are routing AI budgets through the Indian majors, not around them. A softer rupee is an incremental margin tailwind here.

Quick Commerce & Internet — the listing wave reopens

The single most important sector event: Zepto filed an updated DRHP, reigniting the entire quick-commerce complex — Eternal (Zomato) and Swiggy both rebounded on the read-through. Eternal holds >58% food-delivery GOV share and q-comm is now growing faster than food delivery. The strategic divergence is the thing to trade: Blinkit chasing users, Swiggy/Zepto pivoting to profitability and higher AOV. Whoever is right about whether q-comm is a land-grab or a margin business will define three years of returns. Shiprocket's IPO (opens 12 Aug, band ₹92–97, ~₹1,618 cr, still loss-making) is the e-commerce-enablement proxy.

Defence & Renewables — order momentum intact

BEL added ₹541 cr of orders (comms, electro-optics, CBRN); its book sits ~₹73,000 cr against HAL's ~₹2.54 lakh crore — multi-year revenue visibility that justifies the sector's premium, if not always its price. In renewables, Waaree Renewable won a 210 MWp solar EPC order and Bondada Engineering bagged an NTPC 250MW solar + 50MW/200MWh BESS package — the storage attach-rate is quietly becoming the interesting part of the solar trade.

Telecom — ARPU thesis compounding

The July tariff hikes (Airtel +10–21%, Jio +13–27%, Vi +10–23%) are the first in ~3 years, and the Street expects a further 12–15% over the next few months. Airtel is steering ARPU toward ₹300. Read this alongside Reliance's "Project Jupiter" Jio IPO DRHP progress — Jio is dressing up revenue and margins ahead of what would be India's largest-ever offering.

Metals, FMCG, Pharma — the laggards

Tata Steel revenue +14% but profit down ~21% QoQ on softer realisations — steel remains a price-taker in a world where the demand impulse is Chinese and uncertain. ITC profit fell 16% despite a 27% revenue jump — margin compression / mix, and a reminder that staples pricing power is not what it was. Pharma has no fresh catalyst this week but carries a live overhang: the new US tariff architecture (below) and continuing USFDA scrutiny (Sun Pharma's Baska OAI) keep it a stock-picker's, not a sector, trade. Quiet week, little meaningful: cement (post-results lull), diagnostics, hospitals, media, aviation, hotels, REITs and ports — nothing that moves the thesis; noted for completeness.

03Corporate Developments

Paytm is the tape-reader's story: a >₹2,038 cr block saw early backer Elevation Capital pare down (NPS Trust the largest buyer, ~₹413 cr), SAIF also reportedly trimming ~2.3% — and yet the stock hit a 52-week high and Bernstein lifted its target to ₹2,200. Large early-investor exits absorbed at a 52-week high is the signature of a stock that has decisively turned from "story" to "cash-flow" — the profitability inflection is now believed. Reliance confirmed the Jio IPO ("Project Jupiter") is progressing toward India's largest-ever offering, Reliance Retail crossed 20,000 stores, and the Meta AI data-centre partnership at Jamnagar continues to build the compute narrative. On M&A, this was a genuinely light week domestically — the action is primary-market, not deal-market.

Pattern worth naming

The domestic liquidity sponge is reopening — watch for crowding-out. Zepto DRHP, Shiprocket IPO, Aditya Birla Capital's earlier QIP, and the looming Jio and (eventually) Reliance Retail mega-listings mean an enormous supply of paper is queuing up to meet that deep domestic bid. Historically, heavy primary issuance is how a strong secondary market tops out — the same SIP flows that are absorbing FII selling get diverted into IPO allotments. If you are wondering what ends the domestic-bid resilience described above, this is the most likely mechanism: not a sentiment break, but a supply flood. Watch IPO subscription multiples and post-listing performance as your early-warning gauge.

04Rates, Currencies & Commodities

Treat these as one story, because right now they are: oil is the independent variable and everything else is a function of it. Brent's move from the low-$80s back to $88 on Iran/Russia supply fears is simultaneously (a) re-pressuring India's 10Y toward 6.85%, (b) capping the rupee near 95.3 despite a soft dollar, (c) widening the trade deficit (June was already $30.4bn, +59% YoY, oil imports $19.3bn), and (d) threatening the CPI print that keeps the RBI on hold. The DXY has slipped below 100 to a two-month low on the weak US jobs data — which should be rupee-supportive — but the oil bid is overriding the dollar channel. That tension resolves whichever way oil goes next.

Globally the commodity complex is screaming supply stress, not demand strength: gold at a record ~$4,340, silver at an all-time high, and copper through $12,000/tonne for the first time ever on mine disruptions. Record copper into a soft-growth world is not a China-reflation trade — it is a capital-cycle signal (see today's Concept). For India, record gold is inflating the import bill and the CAD ahead of the festival/wedding season, when import demand structurally peaks.

06Opinions Worth Reading

Bloomberg Opinion — "A Tale of Two Bubbles Defining a Third." The argument: AI-stock investors are reaching for 2008 analogies rather than 1999, because the core worry has shifted from valuation to financing — Big Tech moving from cash-funded to debt-funded AI capex. This is the sharpest framing of the AI risk I've seen this week and it deserves your attention: a valuation bubble deflates slowly; a credit-financed capex bubble deflates violently. With hyperscaler 2026 capex now cited near $725bn, the question of how it's funded is the one that matters.

Ruchir Sharma argues AI checks all four of his bubble boxes — over-investment, over-valuation, over-ownership, over-leverage. I'd push back gently: over-ownership and over-valuation are clearly true; over-leverage is becoming true (the Bloomberg point) but isn't yet systemic; and "over-investment" in compute is not obviously mal-investment if the demand is real. The checklist is a useful discipline, but a bubble that "checks every box" has been called for eighteen months while the megacaps compounded — timing is everything and the checklist doesn't give you any.

The through-line across this week's best commentary is concentration risk: every top US company is now AI-levered, so the "market" and "the AI trade" have become the same position. For an India allocator, that is quietly reassuring — Indian megacaps (Reliance, HDFC Bank, SBI) are levered to domestic credit and consumption, not to a single global capex cycle. Diversification you get paid for.

07Global Intelligence

Part A — Markets & Macro

United States. The S&P 500 closed at a record 7,758 Friday, its strongest week since April, powered by AI/tech earnings (a Palantir blowout, AMD) and easing oil intraweek. But the real news was the labour market: July payrolls fell 23,000 — an outright contraction versus +83k expected — with the prior two months revised down a combined 103k, and unemployment ticking to 4.1%. Under new Chair Kevin Warsh (sworn in 22 May, replacing Powell, who stays on as a governor), the Fed held at 3.50–3.75% for a fifth straight meeting on a 9–3 vote — the three dissents all wanting HIGHER rates. Warsh has stripped forward guidance and says he "won't hesitate" on inflation. Markets price ~55% odds of a September hike. Both US and India CPI land Wednesday — the fulcrum of the week.

Where I break with the crowd

A 55% September hike into a −23k payroll print is an internal contradiction, and I'd fade the hike. The Fed is being asked to tighten into a labour market that just went negative, on the basis of an oil price that is a supply shock, not a demand-driven overheating. History says a genuine labour crack beats a commodity-driven inflation blip in the reaction function — Warsh's hawkish rhetoric notwithstanding, a second negative payroll print would collapse the hike odds fast. The asymmetry: if I'm right, the pivot-lower in DM rate expectations is a powerful tailwind for EM and for the rupee, and it's the exact catalyst that brings FII money back to India. If I'm wrong, oil is the reason, and you were long the barrel anyway. The trade is to own duration and EM into Wednesday's CPI, sized for a dovish surprise.

Europe & Japan. The ECB hiked in June (deposit 2.25%) and is signalling a possible September move — synchronised DM tightening, which drains global liquidity and is a headwind for all EM including India. Japan is the sleeper risk: the BoJ is at 1.0%, its highest since 1995, the 10Y JGB is at a multi-decade 2.81%, and the yen is near historic lows with intervention in play. A rising BoJ rate plus intervention is exactly the setup for a carry-trade unwind — the mechanism behind the August 2024 global convulsion — and it's the tail risk to monitor daily.

China. Genuinely improving at the margin: 1Q GDP +5.0%, and PPI turned positive year-on-year for the first time in 41 months, easing the deflation that has haunted the tape. CSI 300 and Hang Seng trade below historical valuations. For India this is a double-edged sword — a stabilising China is commodity-supportive, but cheap Chinese equities are the most direct competitor for the EM allocation dollar that has been leaving India.

Part B — Beyond Markets

The AI capex super-cycle re-accelerated. The Q2 CY2026 hyperscaler prints were all about one number: capex went up, again. Microsoft (~$190bn CY26 guide, Azure AI +123%), Alphabet (~$195–205bn, Cloud +63%), Amazon (~$220bn), Meta (raised, but punished −10% on margin compression). The market's discrimination is telling — it rewarded revenue-proven cloud acceleration (MSFT, GOOG) and punished capex-without-visible-return (META). That is the first sign of the market beginning to price AI spend on ROIC rather than faith. Unverified but high-impact: reports of an Anthropic Series H at a ~$965bn valuation and a confidential IPO filing — extraordinary if true; I would not act on it until confirmed against primary sources.

Semis / geopolitics. Nvidia is winding down the China H20 and developing a Blackwell-based B30A successor — approval of which is a binary US-government catalyst into Nvidia's late-August earnings. Separately, the new US tariff architecture is the India-relevant policy story: the 2 Feb US–India deal set India's rate at 18% (down from a 2025 peak of 50%), but a fresh 10% "forced-labour" tariff on 60 countries (23–24 July) appears to sit alongside it, and the stacking is unclear. Net: a material de-risking from the 50% peak, but residual ambiguity that keeps pharma, textiles, electronics and solar exporters on watch. Bitcoin, for context, is rangebound near $65k — roughly 48% below its October 2025 high, now trading as a rates-sensitive macro asset rather than in a bull phase.

09Concept of the Day

The Capital Cycle

You know the cycle in the abstract; today it's the unifying lens for three of this brief's biggest stories, so let's make it operational. The capital cycle is the idea that returns are driven less by demand than by the supply of capital chasing it. When an industry earns high returns, capital floods in, capacity expands, and — with a lag — those very returns are competed away. When an industry is starved of capital, capacity shrinks, and the survivors eventually earn extraordinary returns on assets nobody else was willing to build. The signal isn't the product; it's where the money is going and, crucially, the lag between the capital and the capacity.

Watch it work in stereo today. Copper through $12,000 and gold at $4,340 are the late phase of a starved cycle: a decade of under-investment in mines meets inelastic supply, and prices go vertical because the capacity simply isn't there — the capital that should have been spent in 2018 wasn't. Now look at AI capex at $725bn: that is the early, euphoric phase — capital flooding in at the top, building capacity whose returns will, if the cycle holds, be competed away with a lag. The tell that the AI cycle is maturing is precisely what we saw this week: the market beginning to punish capex-without-return (Meta −10%) while rewarding proven cash flow. That is the capital cycle's warning light starting to blink.

Application to your book. Overweight the industries where capital has left and supply is constrained (commodities, select shipping/ports, anything the market spent a decade defunding); be disciplined about the industries where capital is arriving in a flood, however good the narrative (AI infrastructure at these capex levels). In India specifically, the capital cycle argues for the capex-starved cyclicals — power, metals, capital goods where a decade of under-build is now meeting a demand upcycle — over the consumer-tech names where private capital has poured in. The best long-term returns hide where the money left, not where it's arriving.
